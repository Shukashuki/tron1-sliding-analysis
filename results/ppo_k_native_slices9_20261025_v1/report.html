Matched PPO training on native nine-slice wheels

Completed four-arm PhysX comparison, 2026-10-25: the new nine-slice policy completed 12,288,000 transitions and strict formal export verification. Both new nominal validation arms completed 2,793 native states and 349 policy decisions. In this single-seed comparison, training on nine rigid slices did not solve support interruptions; both new arms also miss the predeclared jitter-reduction gate. Stable-contact task completion remains unachieved.

This extends the completed frozen-policy wheel-model transfers. Those transfers changed the validation model while keeping the original K actor. This experiment trains a new policy from scratch on the nine-slice model, using the same initial network, K reward arithmetic, remaining 14 reward terms, PPO settings and domain randomization.

What changes

Each original radius-0.127 m, width-0.05 m wheel is represented by nine adjacent rigid axial cylinders. This changes collision decomposition and contact sampling; it introduces no tire elasticity or additional degrees of freedom. Native Coulomb friction is retained. Contact/rest offsets are explicitly 1 mm/0 on both validation geometries. Original mass, center of mass, principal inertia and cylinder-to-body transforms are retained.

Nonlinear combined-slip tire forces remain a separate force-law comparison in the previous report. Their current parameters are a model intervention, not a calibrated TRON1 tire measurement. The existing single-environment external-force implementation is not used for this vectorized training.

Frozen recipe and acceptance

PhysX at 400 Hz, policy at 50 Hz; action-rate weight −0.5; K grounded weight +0.4; nonzero reference β tracking +2. No absolute height gate. Native material DR remains μ∈[0.3,1.2], with one shared wheel draw per environment. Encoder bias, base-COM perturbations and pushes retain their original recipe. Each policy starts from seed 42 and receives 512×24×1000 = 12,288,000 transitions.

At least one wheel must support more than 5 N at every recorded physical sample in 0.02–6.96 s; alternating and single-wheel support are allowed. The acceptance condition remains 100% any-wheel support and zero support gap, alongside the predeclared tracking, speed, β and high-frequency jitter gates. All four rows fail contact continuity; both new-policy rows additionally fail the HF gate. These thresholds were retained without modification.
