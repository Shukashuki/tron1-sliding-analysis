Does smoothing the leg command remove support loss?

Completed inference-time diagnostic, 2026-10-25: spreading each leg position command over eight actual physical applications changed the timing of support interruptions but did not improve continuity. Tracking remained similar, while support fraction and maximum no-support gap became worse. This is a frozen-policy execution intervention, with no new PPO training or reward edits.

Two actual completed whole-body/XY arms · Four diagnostic plots · Complete native analysis.

Why this intervention

In the previous geometry comparison, 34/35 original-cylinder interruptions began on the first physical sample after a policy update. The nine-slice-trained policy on nine slices had 26/32 first-substep onsets. The phase counts for all four published arms motivated a direct command-execution test. Timing correlation alone was not treated as a causal conclusion.

Both new runs use the same normalized original-K actor, same initial mechanical state, original cylinder geometry, μ=0.7, native Coulomb contact, explicit 1 mm/0 offsets, original mass/COM/inertia, PD 500/10, motor limits, 400 Hz physics and 50 Hz policy. No absolute height or flight gate is added. Body/reference β remains nonzero.

The baseline calls the original installed leg-action method unchanged. In the intervention, each processed six-joint leg target moves from its preceding actual command toward the current desired target across eight real physics applications, with an exact final endpoint. The wheel effort commands retain their original hold. Raw action and observation mappings remain unchanged, while subsequent actions may differ through physical state feedback.

Support is evaluated over 0.02–6.96 s, core tracking over 1.44–4 s. The HF ratio uses the unchanged original same-engine 400 Hz PPO baseline and a 0.7 maximum. Both runs pass core/whole XY, speed, reference-β and HF screens, but fail both continuous-support criteria. The ramp retains 98.50% of reference speed with β RMSE 4.37°.

The largest recorded physical leg-target increment, measured as the six-joint L2 norm, falls from 0.87489 to 0.10936 rad. Support interruption count falls from 35 to 29, but total unsupported samples increase. First-substep onsets change from 34/35 to 4/29, with the remaining onsets spread through the window. Maximum measured single-wheel upward normal force rises from approximately 1.86 to 2.11 kN. Fewer events and smaller maximum command increments therefore do not establish more stable contact.

This result rules out treating inference-only target ramping as an established fix for this actor. It does not evaluate a policy trained with the ramp. The stable-contact objective and cross-engine qualification remain incomplete. No threshold has been relaxed: at least one wheel must exceed 5 N at every physical sample, with single-wheel or alternating support allowed.
